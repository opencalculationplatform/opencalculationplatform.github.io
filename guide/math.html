Matrices, symbolic math and solving

Vectors and matrices with units, complex numbers, exact answers with →, and solvers that find, check and explain their answer.

On this pageMatrices and vectorsComplex numbersExact answers with →Integrals, sums and derivativesSolving in a formulaThe equation solverCurve fitting and interpolationRandom numbers

Matrices and vectors

Math › Matrix inserts one: pick the rows and columns on the grid. Add row and Add column grow the one you are editing. Elements may have units, and even be texts (for a table of sections).

All matrix functions (transpose, inverse, det, identity, augment, stack, submatrix, sort, eigenvalues…) are in the function reference.

Complex numbers

Write 3+4i or 4j, with units if you like. Roots, powers and logarithms of negative numbers give complex answers.

Exact answers with →

End a formula with → (Calculus › Symbolic) instead of = to get an exact, symbolic answer: simplified, expanded, factored, integrated or solved.

The keywords (expand, factor, solve, substitute, series, collect, parfrac, assume, float) are in the function reference.

Integrals, sums and derivatives

With =, integrals, sums and derivatives are worked out numerically; with →, exactly.

Calculus

Σ
Finite sum: index = first value, last value at the top, then the term
∫
Definite integral, evaluated numerically: lower and upper bound, integrand, then the variable after d
d/dx
Derivative: with = its slope at the variable’s value above; with → its formula (Ctrl+.)
lim
Limit, with →: lim x→0 sin(x)/x → 1; x→∞ for infinity
∫ dx
Integral without limits, with →: its formula (an antiderivative)

Solving in a formula

Functions such as root, minimize, maximize, lsolve, polyroots and eigenvals solve inside any formula.

The equation solver

Insert › Equation solver (or Calculus › Equation solver; on the phone, Solve on the bar) adds a solver card. Type the equation and the unknown; the answer becomes a variable for the formulas below.

  • Modes (Settings): numerical (from a guess or between bounds), analytical (exact), root search (every sign change in an interval), optimise (minimum or maximum, with constraints, integer or list choices) and least squares (best fit).
  • Systems: several equations and unknowns together.
  • Check at the solution: each equation is put back with the answer, so you see that it holds.
  • Sensitivity: how much the answer changes when each input changes by 1 %.
  • Sweep: solve again over a range of one input, as a table.

Linear systems can also be solved exactly in one formula: solve([eq1; eq2], [x; y]) →. Differential equations: rkfixed and Rkadapt.

Curve fitting and interpolation

Curve fitting

line
The least-squares line through points: line(vx, vy) gives its intercept and slope
slope
The slope of the least-squares line through points (vx, vy), e.g. a stiffness from test data
intercept
Where the least-squares line through points (vx, vy) crosses x = 0
polyfit
The least-squares polynomial of degree n through points: polyfit(vx, vy, n) gives its coefficients, the constant first
linterp
Linear interpolation in a table: linterp(vx, vy, x), the x values increasing
interp
Smooth interpolation by a natural cubic spline: interp(lspline(vx, vy), vx, vy, x)

Random numbers

rnd(x), runif(m, a, b) and rnorm(m, μ, σ) give random numbers. Each line keeps its own sequence, so values stay put until you change that line (or SEED).
